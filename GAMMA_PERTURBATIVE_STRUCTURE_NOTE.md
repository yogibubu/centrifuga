# Gamma Perturbative Structure Note

This note fixes the first perturbative and tensorial organization of the
`gamma` branch, i.e. the next vibration-rotation correction after `alpha`.
It is not intended as a finished derivation. Its role is to define the
observable, the likely tensor hierarchy behind it, and the first structural
questions that must be answered before any symbolic implementation.

## 1. Spectroscopic observable

For a vibrational state labeled by occupation numbers `n_r`, the rotational
constants may be written schematically as

`B_alpha(v) = B_e - sum_r alpha_r (n_r + 1/2) + ...`

The next branch is the quadratic occupation dependence, schematically

`B_alpha_gamma(v) = B_e - sum_r alpha_r (n_r + 1/2)
                    + sum_rs gamma_rs (n_r + 1/2)(n_s + 1/2) + ...`

with the obvious specialization to the individual rotational constants
`A_v, B_v, C_v` or to the symmetry-adapted readout in the special rotor
limits.

The `gamma` object should therefore be read as:

- the coefficient of the quadratic vibrational-state dependence of the
  rotational constants;
- the natural successor of `alpha` on the vibration-rotation branch;
- the analogue, on the `B_v` side, of moving from lower-order to
  higher-order effective observables on the centrifugal-distortion side.

## 2. Tensorial starting point

The natural geometric starting point is the expansion of the inverse inertia
tensor around equilibrium,

`mu(Q) = mu0 + sum_k mu1_k Q_k
        + 1/2 sum_kl mu2_kl Q_k Q_l
        + 1/6 sum_klm mu3_klm Q_k Q_l Q_m + ...`

In the CeDiTT language:

- `mu1` is the first-level object already used for quartics, sextics, and
  now `alpha`;
- `c1` is the corresponding practical first-level working quantity;
- `zeta` carries the Coriolis structure;
- cubic force constants `phi3` provide the first explicit anharmonic input.

The first question for `gamma` is therefore exactly analogous to the one
already solved for quartics/sextics:

- does the observable still close on the first-level sector generated by
  `mu1/c1`, `zeta`, and the usual low-order anharmonic data?
- or does the first genuinely new tensor level already enter?

## 3. What is already known from the alpha branch

The present backend shows that `alpha` can be written in the CeDiTT language
using:

- `c1` reconstructed from `mu1`,
- `zeta`,
- semi-diagonal cubic force constants as the standard anharmonic input,
- symmetry-adapted projections for asymmetric, symmetric, and linear limits.

Thus `alpha` belongs, at least in its standard branch, to the same
first-level tensorial world as the standard sextic geometry sector.

This makes `gamma` the first natural place where the following question must
be answered explicitly:

- does the first-level closure persist one step further on the
  vibration-rotation branch?

## 4. Three structural scenarios for gamma

At this stage there are three logically distinct scenarios.

### Scenario A: first-level closure persists

`gamma` is expressible entirely in terms of:

- `mu1/c1`,
- `zeta`,
- cubic force constants,
- and possibly quartic-force information,

without any genuinely new geometric tensor level beyond the first one.

In this case the `alpha -> gamma` step would resemble the
`quartic -> standard sextic` step: a richer observable, but still within the
same tensorial closure.

### Scenario B: first breakdown enters already at gamma

`gamma` requires the second geometric response,

`mu2 = B(mu1) - N`

and therefore the intrinsic second-level tensor `N` enters explicitly.

In this case `gamma` would play, on the vibration-rotation branch, a role
structurally analogous to the first quartic beyond-standard corrections on the
centrifugal-distortion branch.

### Scenario C: mixed structure

The standard or dominant part of `gamma` closes on the first-level sector, but
the first controlled breakdown appears as an additional separable correction.

This is the most plausible working hypothesis:

- a standard `gamma_std` built from first-level data;
- a first higher-level correction `delta gamma_break` involving `N` or the
  next nontrivial closure.

This would mirror the CeDiTT philosophy most closely.

## 5. Minimal perturbative sources to inspect

Without committing yet to the final formulas, the perturbative ingredients
that may contribute to `gamma` are expected to include:

- repeated first-level geometry (`mu1/c1`);
- Coriolis couplings `zeta`;
- cubic force constants `phi3`;
- possibly quartic force constants `phi4` in the first explicit anharmonic
  extension;
- if required, the second geometric response `mu2` and its split
  `B(mu1) - N`.

The structural task is not just to list terms, but to classify them into:

- first-level closures;
- explicit anharmonic input of the standard branch;
- genuinely new tensor levels.

## 6. Expected organization of the derivation

The derivation of `gamma` should be organized in the same way as CeDiTT:

1. define the observable branch cleanly at the spectroscopic level;
2. expand the rotational block in the perturbative Hamiltonian;
3. identify which expectation values or effective-Hamiltonian coefficients
   produce the quadratic occupation dependence;
4. rewrite the result in the tensor language `mu1/c1`, `zeta`, `phi3`, ...;
5. isolate the largest standard closure;
6. test whether a first controlled breakdown term can be defined separately.

The crucial design principle is that the final observable should be written,
as far as possible, in nonresonant assembled form, even if intermediate
contributions are channel-resolved and may look resonance-prone.

## 7. Symmetry and special rotor limits

The `gamma` branch must be symmetry-adapted from the beginning.

The same architecture already built for `alpha` should be reused:

- asymmetric-top readout in the generic case;
- axial projection for symmetric tops;
- scalar perpendicular/parallel readout for linear molecules;
- canonical handling of degenerate mode subspaces through projectors rather
  than ad hoc branch logic.

This is important because `gamma` is more likely than `alpha` to involve mode
pair structure explicitly.

## 8. Immediate technical consequence for Paper 4

Paper 4 should not jump directly from the current `alpha` backend to coding
`gamma`.

Instead, the sequence should be:

1. finish the structural control of `alpha`;
2. define the standard tensorial sector of `gamma`;
3. decide whether `N` enters already in the standard observable or only in the
   first controlled breakdown;
4. only then generate the formulas symbolically and implement the backend.

## 9. Working conclusion

The safest current statement is:

- `alpha` is already in the first-level CeDiTT tensor language;
- `gamma` is the next observable of the same branch;
- it is not yet known whether the first-level closure survives completely or
  whether the first genuinely new tensor level enters already there;
- the correct strategy is therefore to formulate `gamma` as
  `standard sector + first controlled breakdown`, exactly as was done for the
  centrifugal-distortion branch.

This gives Paper 4 a precise theoretical target without pretending that the
final algebra is already known.
