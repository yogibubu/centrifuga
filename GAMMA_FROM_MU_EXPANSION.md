# Gamma from the Expansion of mu(Q)

This note switches to the direct derivational viewpoint. Instead of starting
from channel taxonomies, it starts from the expansion of the rotational block
itself and asks:

- where does the linear vibrational correction `alpha` come from?
- what is the next coefficient, i.e. `gamma`, in the same expansion?

This is the closest analogue, on the vibration-rotation branch, of the way
the higher-order quartic branch was built by looking directly at the next
perturbative layer of the relevant observable.

## 1. Rotational block to expand

At the effective level, the rotational Hamiltonian may be written as

`H_rot(Q) = sum_{ab} mu_ab(Q) J_a J_b`

with

`mu(Q) = mu0 + sum_i mu1_i Q_i
        + 1/2 sum_{ij} mu2_ij Q_i Q_j
        + 1/6 sum_{ijk} mu3_ijk Q_i Q_j Q_k + ...`

The vibrational dependence of the rotational constants comes from taking
expectation values of this operator in vibrational states and then including
the usual perturbative corrections from the transformed rovibrational
Hamiltonian.

If one ignores, for the moment, Coriolis mixing and anharmonic corrections,
the direct expectation value gives the first schematic structure:

`<v|mu(Q)|v>
 = mu0
 + sum_i mu1_i <v|Q_i|v>
 + 1/2 sum_{ij} mu2_ij <v|Q_i Q_j|v>
 + ...`

For harmonic number states:

- `<v|Q_i|v> = 0`,
- `<v|Q_i Q_j|v> = delta_ij (n_i + 1/2) / omega_i`.

So already at this naive level the first nonzero state dependence comes from
the quadratic term in `Q`, i.e. from `mu2`.

This is the first sharp difference from the CeDiTT sextic story:

- quartic and sextic standard distortion could be reorganized without taking
  primitive `mu2` as a starting object;
- for vibration-rotation corrections, the direct state dependence of the
  rotational block itself points immediately to `mu2`.

## 2. Why alpha does not force primitive mu2 in the final formula

The fact that the direct expectation value sees `mu2` does **not** yet mean
that the final `alpha` observable must be written with primitive `mu2`.

Indeed, the current backend and the Gaussian-style formula show that `alpha`
can be rewritten in first-level language:

- `c1 ~ mu1`,
- `zeta`,
- explicit cubic-force input.

So the lesson is the same as in CeDiTT:

- the raw perturbative route suggests a higher derivative;
- the final assembled observable may still allow that derivative to be
  eliminated in favor of lower-level closures plus explicit anharmonic input.

Therefore the right question for `gamma` is not:

- "does `mu2` appear somewhere in a raw expansion?"

but:

- "after assembling the observable completely, can the `mu2` dependence be
  removed again, as happens for `alpha`?"

## 3. Where alpha sits in the direct expansion

Write the vibrationally corrected rotational tensor schematically as

`mu_v = mu0 + sum_i A_i (n_i + 1/2) + ...`

Then `alpha_i` is just the first coefficient tensor of this state expansion.

From the direct `mu(Q)` expansion, `A_i` receives contributions from:

1. the diagonal expectation value of `mu2_ii`;
2. second-order mixing generated by the linear term `mu1_i Q_i`;
3. Coriolis couplings;
4. explicit anharmonic corrections from the vibrational wavefunction /
   effective Hamiltonian.

This explains why `alpha` can end up in first-level language even though the
direct expectation route points to `mu2`: the combination

- direct `mu2`,
- plus second-order `mu1 * mu1`,
- plus wavefunction/anharmonic pieces

can reorganize into a final expression using only:

- `mu1/c1`,
- `zeta`,
- `phi3`.

## 4. The next coefficient: where gamma must come from

Now write the expansion one step further:

`mu_v = mu0
      + sum_i A_i (n_i + 1/2)
      + sum_{ij} G_ij (n_i + 1/2)(n_j + 1/2)
      + ...`

The tensor `G_ij` is the precursor of `gamma_ij`.

At the direct perturbative level, `G_ij` can only come from:

1. one order higher in the expectation-value expansion of `mu(Q)`,
   i.e. terms probing at least cubic/quartic dependence on the normal
   coordinates;
2. one order higher in the wavefunction/effective-Hamiltonian correction to
   the expectation value;
3. bilinear recombination of lower-order first-level couplings after the
   contact transformation / perturbative assembly.

In practical terms this means:

- `gamma` must be sought one level above the `alpha` assembly problem;
- but it does **not** follow automatically that a primitive object beyond
  first level survives in the final answer.

## 5. Direct derivative reading of the hierarchy

From the direct expansion of `mu(Q)` the sequence looks like:

- equilibrium rotational constants:
  `mu0`
- first vibrational correction branch:
  raw dependence on `mu2`, assembled finally as `alpha`
- second vibrational correction branch:
  raw dependence on `mu3`, `mu4`, and/or iterated lower-order couplings,
  assembled finally as `gamma`

So the derivative hierarchy seen naively from `mu(Q)` is:

- `alpha`: starts at the level of the second response;
- `gamma`: starts one level above that.

But CeDiTT has already taught the relevant lesson:

- the naive derivative level is not the same thing as the primitive tensor
  level of the final observable.

This is exactly why the direct expansion is useful: it tells us where the
observable starts perturbatively, but not yet which tensors survive after
reorganization.

## 6. Immediate consequence for gamma

This direct viewpoint leads to a much sharper reformulation of the open
question:

1. `alpha` is the first assembled state coefficient, and its final formula
   can be written in first-level language despite the raw appearance of
   `mu2`;
2. `gamma` is the next assembled state coefficient;
3. therefore the real question is whether the one-order-higher raw derivative
   content can also be eliminated down to the same first-level language, or
   whether a genuinely new tensor level survives.

In other words:

- the raw expansion says `gamma` sits one perturbative step above `alpha`;
- the CeDiTT question is whether that extra step is still reducible to the
  first-level sector or not.

## 7. Most likely direct decomposition

From this viewpoint the most likely final organization is:

`gamma = gamma_std + delta_gamma_break`

with

- `gamma_std` assembled from the same first-level building blocks already
  visible in `alpha`,
- `delta_gamma_break` measuring the first higher-level piece that cannot be
  eliminated.

This is the direct analogue of:

- standard sector + first breakdown

on the centrifugal-distortion branch.

## 8. What should be derived next

The next derivation should therefore not start from fully abstract channel
names. It should start from the state expansion:

1. write the coefficient of `(n_i + 1/2)` and identify the assembled source of
   `alpha`;
2. write the coefficient of `(n_i + 1/2)(n_j + 1/2)` and identify the first
   assembled source of `gamma`;
3. only then ask which parts of that coefficient can be rewritten with:
   - `mu1/c1`,
   - `zeta`,
   - `phi3`,
   and which parts require genuinely new tensor content.

This is the right bridge between:

- the direct perturbative expansion,
- and the CeDiTT-style tensor classification.
