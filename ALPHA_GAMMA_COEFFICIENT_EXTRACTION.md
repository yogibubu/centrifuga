# Alpha / Gamma Coefficient Extraction from the State Expansion

This note applies the common Paper 2 / Paper 4 strategy directly to the
vibration-rotation branch. The aim is to extract, at the formal level, the
coefficients that define:

- `alpha_i`,
- `gamma_ij`,

from the vibrational-state expansion of the rotational tensor.

This is not yet the reduced CeDiTT formula. It is the direct observable-level
starting point from which the standard-sector reduction should be attempted.

## 1. Observable to expand

Write the vibrationally corrected inverse-inertia tensor as the state-dependent
effective object

`mu^(eff)(v)`.

The rotational Hamiltonian in state `v` is then

`H_rot^(eff)(v) = sum_ab mu_ab^(eff)(v) J_a J_b`.

The rotational constants are the corresponding projected components of
`mu^(eff)(v)`.

For the present purpose it is enough to expand `mu^(eff)(v)` itself as

`mu^(eff)(v)
 = mu^(0)
 + sum_i M_i (n_i + 1/2)
 + sum_{ij} M_ij (n_i + 1/2)(n_j + 1/2)
 + ...`

Then:

- `alpha_i` is the spectroscopic projection of `M_i`,
- `gamma_ij` is the spectroscopic projection of `M_ij`.

So the whole problem reduces to identifying `M_i` and `M_ij`.

## 2. Direct expansion before reduction

Start from the bare coordinate dependence

`mu(Q) = mu0
       + sum_i mu1_i Q_i
       + 1/2 sum_{ij} mu2_ij Q_i Q_j
       + 1/6 sum_{ijk} mu3_ijk Q_i Q_j Q_k
       + 1/24 sum_{ijkl} mu4_ijkl Q_i Q_j Q_k Q_l
       + ...`

The effective state tensor `mu^(eff)(v)` is obtained by combining:

1. direct expectation values of `mu(Q)` in the vibrational state;
2. perturbative corrections to the vibrational wavefunction;
3. contact-transformation / effective-Hamiltonian assembly terms;
4. Coriolis and anharmonic couplings.

At this stage, keep these sources separate.

## 3. Bare expectation-value contribution

If one looks only at direct expectation values in a harmonic number state,

`mu^(dir)(v) = <v|mu(Q)|v>`,

then:

- odd powers of `Q` do not contribute directly;
- even powers do.

Using the harmonic moments schematically:

- `<Q_i> = 0`,
- `<Q_i Q_j> = delta_ij q_i`,
- `<Q_i Q_j Q_k> = 0`,
- `<Q_i Q_j Q_k Q_l> = fourth-order moments`,

with `q_i proportional to (n_i + 1/2)/omega_i`,
one gets

`mu^(dir)(v)
 = mu0
 + 1/2 sum_i mu2_ii q_i
 + 1/24 sum_{ijkl} mu4_ijkl <Q_i Q_j Q_k Q_l>
 + ...`

Thus, at the raw direct level:

- the coefficient linear in `(n_i + 1/2)` starts from `mu2`;
- the coefficient quadratic in occupations starts from the quartic expectation
  sector and therefore from `mu4` and/or products generated by lower-order
  perturbative assembly.

This is only the raw derivative reading, not yet the final tensor content.

## 4. Definition of the extracted coefficients

The cleanest formal definition is:

`M_i = coeff[ mu^(eff)(v), (n_i + 1/2) ]`

`M_ij = coeff[ mu^(eff)(v), (n_i + 1/2)(n_j + 1/2) ]`

where `coeff[.,.]` means the coefficient of the indicated polynomial in the
 vibrational occupations.

Then:

`alpha_i = P_rot(M_i)`

`gamma_ij = P_rot(M_ij)`

with `P_rot` the appropriate spectroscopic projection:

- asymmetric-top readout in the generic case,
- symmetry-adapted projection for symmetric and linear limits.

This is the direct extraction step, independent of any later reduction to
first-level tensor language.

## 5. Schematic source decomposition of M_i

At the observable level, the linear coefficient tensor may be written as

`M_i = M_i^(dir,2) + M_i^(mix,11) + M_i^(Cor) + M_i^(anh) + ...`

where:

- `M_i^(dir,2)` is the direct contribution from `mu2`;
- `M_i^(mix,11)` is the second-order assembled contribution from two `mu1`
  couplings;
- `M_i^(Cor)` is the Coriolis contribution;
- `M_i^(anh)` is the explicit anharmonic contribution.

The existing `alpha` backend shows that this full `M_i` can be rewritten in
terms of:

- `mu1/c1`,
- `zeta`,
- `phi3`.

So the direct `mu2` dependence does not survive as a primitive object in the
final reduced `alpha` formula.

## 6. Schematic source decomposition of M_ij

By exact analogy, the quadratic coefficient tensor should be written first as

`M_ij = M_ij^(dir,4)
      + M_ij^(mix,22)
      + M_ij^(mix,13)
      + M_ij^(mix,1111)
      + M_ij^(Cor)
      + M_ij^(anh)
      + ...`

The labels here are purely schematic:

- `dir,4` means direct contribution from the quartic expectation layer;
- `mix,22` means assembled contributions involving second-response objects
  twice;
- `mix,13` means mixed third/first response structure;
- `mix,1111` means closures generated entirely from first-level couplings
  after perturbative assembly;
- `Cor` and `anh` collect Coriolis and explicit anharmonic pieces.

This is the right place to start, because it mirrors the way one isolates
higher-order quartic observables before asking what their final tensor content
really is.

## 7. The actual reduction problem

Once `M_i` and `M_ij` are defined this way, the CeDiTT-style question is:

- which parts of `M_i` and `M_ij` can be rewritten entirely in first-level
  language?
- which parts can be absorbed into a standard anharmonic extension?
- which part is the first irreducible remainder?

So the reduction problem is:

`M_i  ->  M_i^(std)  + delta M_i^(break?)`

`M_ij -> M_ij^(std) + delta M_ij^(break)`

with the known empirical fact that for `alpha`

`delta M_i^(break?) = 0`

within the currently exposed standard branch.

The open problem is whether `delta M_ij^(break)` vanishes or not.

## 8. Consequence for the next derivation step

The next derivation should therefore not begin from abstract channels alone.
It should begin by writing explicitly:

1. the occupation-polynomial expansion of `mu^(eff)(v)`;
2. the coefficient tensor `M_i`;
3. the coefficient tensor `M_ij`;
4. only then the attempt to reduce `M_i` and `M_ij` to:
   - first-level closures,
   - standard anharmonic pieces,
   - first controlled breakdown.

This is the exact analogue, on the `alpha/gamma` branch, of the direct
observable-first method that should also be used for the higher-order quartic
paper.
